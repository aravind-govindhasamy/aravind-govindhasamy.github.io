It is official: **I have successfully secured my PhD admission at PSG College of Arts & Science (PSGCAS), Coimbatore!**

Standing on the campus today with my official admission confirmed, I felt a quiet sense of triumph. But if today taught me anything, it's that securing a seat in research isn't just about qualifying academically or defense presentations—it is an endurance test of patience, bureaucracy, and determination.

Here is the story of how a stressful day turned into one of the most fulfilling milestones of my academic and professional journey.

## The unexpected roadblock: A missing seal and signature

Everything was set. I walked into the admission center with all my academic mark sheets, certificates, identity proofs, and experience documents neatly organized in folders.

Then came the verification step.

The administrative officer reviewed the service letter from **2CQR** (my first company, where my professional journey began). They paused, pointed at the bottom of the page, and delivered the roadblock: **The service letter was rejected.**

Because it lacked an official physical seal and signature, it didn't satisfy the university's strict compliance criteria.

In that moment, a sudden wave of exhaustion and frustration hit me. After all the months of preparing research proposals, balancing full-stack development, and planning my academic roadmap, a missing stamp on my foundational work experience document threatened to stall everything right at the finish line.

## Frantic calls, fresh documentation, and pushing forward

I knew I couldn't throw away months of effort over a missing seal.

Instead of panicking, I took a deep breath and focused on solutions:

1. I immediately got on the phone to coordinate with both **2CQR** (to rectify the missing physical seal/signature on my initial service letter) and my current company's HR team.
2. Both teams acted swiftly to reissue, sign, and stamp the required service verification documents.
3. With all official service letters properly sealed and submitted alongside my current company credentials, the compliance requirements were finally satisfied.

Running back and forth between calls, printers, document verification counters, and administrative desks under the hot Coimbatore sun was mentally and physically draining. By mid-afternoon, I felt completely depleted.

But I kept telling myself: _This is just the final test of my commitment._

## The moment of confirmation

By the end of the day, with properly sealed service letters from both 2CQR and my current company submitted and verified, the administrative team gave the final nod.

The receipt was generated, the admission ledger was signed, and I was officially registered as a PhD Scholar at PSGCAS Coimbatore.

The physical exhaustion instantly vanished, replaced by an overwhelming feeling of relief and gratitude.

## Lessons from the day

Looking back at today's roller coaster, I gained a few valuable lessons:

1. **Paperwork requires as much focus as research:** Administrative compliance is strict for a reason. Ensuring every single document—especially service letters from your past companies like 2CQR and your current employer—has physical signatures and seals saves hours of anxiety.
2. **Resilience is part of the PhD mindset:** The research journey will test patience far beyond code bugs and data analysis. Staying calm under unexpected pressure is the first real research skill we develop.
3. **Gratitude for support systems:** Special thanks to the teams at 2CQR and my current company for acting swiftly to provide the properly sealed documents without delay when time was running out.

## What's Next?

With admission officially secured at PSGCAS, the real work now begins.

I am eager to dive deep into my research focusing on **AI-driven RFID inventory optimization, ERPNext integrations, and IoT enterprise systems**—fields that directly stem from the work I started back at 2CQR and continue to expand today.

Balancing full-stack industry engineering with doctoral research won't be easy, but overcoming today's final hurdle proved that I am ready for whatever comes next.
